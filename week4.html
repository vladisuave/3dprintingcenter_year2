Our Lab Rules [~ 3 min] Review this slide each week prior to the lesson content

Safety: hands off printers. The printer has a hot nozzle and moving parts that can pinch. The printer will keep moving, even if something is in its path.

Kind words and suggestions: everyone's design is their own artistic vision. Helpful ideas are welcome ('Have you tried...?', 'I like how...'), but we do not put down others designs.

Questions at the end: many of your questions will be answered in the lesson material. We answer any questions you still have at the end, this allows us to have more build time.

White filament: every print comes out white. Painting can be done at home or in week 6.

Stay on task: if you are off task you may be asked to go back to your classroom.

Locked items stay locked: some parts are locked on purpose so they stay the right size. Do not click the padlock to unlock them. If something will not move, it is supposed to stay put.

Mod Your Robot: 3 Steps [~5 min]

Optional LIVE DEMO on the projector while students watch. Chromebooks stay closed until after questions.

1. Drag: drag a yellow mod, use Ctrl + D for more copies of the same piece. Drag them to a blue part.

2. Overlap: the mod must sink into the blue part a little. A mod that only sits next to it, with a gap, prints as a loose piece.

3. Side check: spin the view. Faces dragged onto the head land on top, which is right. A hand or foot dragged onto the end of an arm can land on top and hang off the edge in the air. Click it and press D to drop it to the Workplane.

Tips

Do not press D on a face pieces: they would sink to the floor inside the head.

No grouping this week. Tinkercad will not group a locked part, and it does not need to: the slicer joins overlapping pieces into one print.

Mods may need supports when they print.

Head Ideas [~2 min] Leave up during building time

Every head starts from the same locked head block with a socket on the bottom (gray in the pictures). The socket snaps onto the ball on the robot neck.

The head prints lying flat, so the top of the block on screen is the face.

Raised shapes on top print cleanly: eyes, a nose, a grille mouth.

Ears, horns and antennas go on the top edge and must overlap the head. No grouping needed. If one hangs off the edge, press D.

The head block is 34 mm wide, 30 mm tall and 10 mm thick, a little oversized on purpose so there is room to design.

Keep everything at least 2 mm thick so it does not snap off, and keep the finished head within about 45 x 45 mm so it is not top-heavy on the spring.

Sockets and Joints Guide [BACKUP SLIDE]

Every connection has two halves: a ball (joint) and a cup (socket). The ball snaps into the cup.

These are all in Tinkercad: Shapes panel, Hardware, Connectors. They are scaling locked so the halves always fit.

Our robot uses the Support Ball and Support Socket. The Support versions have a built-in angled support so they print lying flat without extra support material.

Ball and Socket: the original pair. They swivel every way but may need supports depending on how they are turned.

Angled Ball: the ball sits on a bent neck, so the part starts out bent. Good for elbows, knees or a tilted head.

Lock Ball: designed to sit tighter once snapped in, so it holds a pose better. Good for legs.

Spinning Hub (pairs with the Spinning Wheel): turns around one axis only, like a wheel. Good for wheels, propellers or a spinning hat.

Socket Cutout: a hole shape. Group it into a thick part to clear space, then place the socket inside.

These uses come from how each part is shaped. Test-print a pair before relying on a new connector type.

Mod Helper [BACKUP SLIDE]

Pull up for individuals or place in stand ups on tables.

Hanging in the air: a mod dragged onto the end of a part can land on top and stick out past the edge. D drops it to the Workplane. Do not use D on a face that sits on top of the head.

Not touching: a gap means the mod prints as a separate loose piece. Nudge it with the arrow keys until it overlaps.

Library moved: the yellow mods are not locked, so they can be dragged. Ctrl + Z puts it back.

Shift + drag a corner resizes evenly. Keep mods at least 2 mm thick so they do not snap off.

Before You Print: Robot Parts [ADULT PREP SLIDE]

Not for students. Use when sending heads and limbs to print after class.

The workplane is not the printer bed. Every solid object in the design exports unless you export only selected shapes.

Select: drag a box around the head block and the 4 limbs, starting from empty workplane. Locked parts can be box-selected even though they cannot be moved. The selection box should not touch the yellow library, the labels or the orange body. Then Export and choose The selected shapes.

Why no grouping: the blue parts are locked, and Tinkercad will not group a locked part. Mods that overlap a blue part are joined into one solid piece by the slicer.

Supports: the head, limbs and balls print flat without supports. Mods sticking out to the side may need them. Tree (organic) supports pop off most easily.

Open the first export in the slicer: you should see 1 head and 4 limbs, with each mod attached and no loose pieces.

The bodies are all the same, so print them ahead of time and separately.

Assembly Day: Build Your Bot [~10 min] Use when the prints are back

Hand out each student's body, head and 4 limbs. Take off any supports before class.

1. Paint first: paint pens on the head, limbs and body. Keep paint off the balls and out of the sockets, or the joints get sticky and tight. Let it dry before snapping together.

2. Head on: line up the socket under the head with the ball on the neck and press straight down until it pops on.

3. Snap limbs: arms go in the side sockets, legs in the bottom sockets. Push each ball straight in.

Test it: push the head down gently. The spring should push it back up. Every joint should swivel.

A ball that will not go in: check for paint or a support bit in the socket. A loose joint: the ball printed small, so a dab of glue holds it in one pose.
