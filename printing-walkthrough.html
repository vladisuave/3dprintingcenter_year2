STL to Print

Bambu Studio → SD card → P1S

Print queue guide. Audience: adults who run the print queue.

The route: slice STL files on the Mac in Bambu Studio, export a .gcode.3mf, copy it to the SD card, put the card in a P1S, and start the print from the printer.

You Need

3 / 29

Gather: your STL files, a MacBook with Bambu Studio installed, the SD card from the printer in its dongle (the dongle plugs into the MacBook), and a P1S printer.

Drag the STLs In

SLICESTEP 2

Open Bambu Studio. Drag your STL files from Finder straight into the window (or use File > Import, shortcut Cmd+I).

If Studio asks whether to treat multiple files as one object or as separate parts, choose separate objects.

In the Prepare tab, top of the left panel: 1) Printer must be Bambu Lab P1S 0.4 nozzle. 2) Plate type must be Textured PEI Plate (that is what is physically on the printer). 3) Filament: pick the Bambu PLA Matte, white.

Check the Settings

SLICESTEP 4
6 / 29

Filament: Bambu PLA Matte, white.

Process: 0.20mm Standard.

Support: tick the Support checkbox (auto-generated supports).

These are our standard settings for every print job in the queue.

Press A to Arrange

SLICESTEP 5
7 / 29

Click on the empty part of the plate and press A (or click the Arrange icon in the toolbar). Studio spreads the parts out so nothing overlaps and everything is on the plate.

If any part sits half off the plate, move it back on.

Slice Plate

SLICESTEP 6
8 / 29

Click Slice plate (bottom right). The progress bar runs for a few seconds. When it finishes, the button changes to Export and Print.

Sliced!

SLICESTEP 7
9 / 29

After slicing, the estimated print time and filament weight appear. Check the time is reasonable for the amount of parts on the plate.

Scrub the Preview

SLICESTEP 8
10 / 29

Click the Preview tab and drag the layer slider at the side of the viewport.

1) Everything is on the plate.

2) Supports (teal) appear under overhangs.

3) The first layer is solid and covers each part's base.

If anything looks wrong, go back to Prepare, fix it, and slice again.

Export Sliced File

EXPORTSTEP 9
Photos: Bambu Lab Wiki (wiki.bambulab.com)
11 / 29

1) Click the small arrow next to the green Print plate button (top right, after slicing).

2) Choose Export plate sliced file. A Save prompt opens next.

This writes a .gcode.3mf, the file the printer reads.

Save It Somewhere Easy

EXPORTSTEP 10
12 / 29

Save the file to somewhere you will find it, like the Desktop. The name should end in .gcode.3mf.

Only the .gcode.3mf will print. An .stl or a plain .3mf will not.

The P1S has no hot-swap SD card, so always eject it from the printer menu first. Use the arrow pad on the printer: go to the Settings (gear) tab, SD Card, choose Eject, confirm Yes.

Only do this on a printer that is not printing.

14 / 29

The slot is on top of the control panel housing, just above the screen. Press the card in with a fingertip, release, and it springs out. Pull it free.

Only after the screen has said it is safe to remove (previous slide).

Card + Dongle into the Mac

TRANSFERSTEP 13
15 / 29

Put the SD card into the dongle and plug the dongle into the MacBook. A drive named something like SD CARD or NO NAME shows up in Finder.

Drag the File onto the Card

TRANSFERSTEP 14
Drop it on the card itself, not inside a folder
16 / 29

In Finder, drag the .gcode.3mf onto the SD card (or open the card and drop it inside). Wait for the copy to finish.

The file must sit at the top level (root) of the card, not inside any folder, or the printer will not list it.

If the card already holds older .gcode.3mf files you can leave them or delete them to keep the list short on the printer screen.

Eject in Finder First

TRANSFERSTEP 15
17 / 29

Click the small eject icon next to SD CARD in the Finder sidebar (or drag the card to the Trash) and wait until it disappears.

Only then unplug the dongle. Pulling the card early can corrupt the file.

Slide the card into the same slot and push gently until it clicks and stays in. Check the printer screen shows the SD card (Settings, SD Card).

Quick Pre-flight

PRINTSTEP 17
19 / 29

Before starting: the textured PEI plate is on the bed and clean (no fingerprints or leftover prints) and white Bambu Matte PLA is loaded and feeds properly.

Enough Filament?

PRINTSTEP 18
20 / 29

Eyeball the roll before you print. A full roll is 1000 g; the job size is the grams shown in Bambu Studio after slicing (the Sliced! slide).

Non-AMS printer and the roll looks too small: take that roll out (Unload Filament slide) and put it in one of the AMS slots on an AMS printer. The AMS switches to another roll of the same filament by itself when one runs out.

AMS printer: just make sure the rolls added together are enough for the job.

On the printer, go to the folder tab (bottom icon on the left of the screen). Use the arrows to select your .gcode.3mf and press OK to start. The printer will heat up and home itself before the first layer, which takes a few minutes.

The screen is a small display with an arrow pad, not a touchscreen.

22 / 29

If the first layer looks wrong, stop right away: press the pause button (the || button on the printer) and choose Stop on the screen, confirming when asked. The pause popup offers Resume and Stop.

Then wait for the plate to cool, remove the failed print, clean the plate and start again.

Verify this against a real printer: the menu wording is from Bambu forum posts, not the wiki.

Watch the First Layer

PRINTSTEP 21
23 / 29

Stay for the first layer (about 3-5 minutes after heating). Good: smooth, even lines that touch each other and stick to the plate.

Bad: gaps, lines peeling up, or stringy spaghetti. If you see it, stop the print from the printer screen, clean the plate and try again.

Use this when swapping rolls on a printer without an AMS.

On the printer screen go to Controls (gear/arrows tab), find Feeding and choose Unload. The nozzle heats up, the filament retracts, then pull the filament out of the PTFE tube at the back and take the roll off the holder.

1) Push the filament into the back PTFE tube until it reaches the extruder.

2) On the printer screen choose Load.

3) Wait for the nozzle to heat (about 250 C), then push the filament in by hand.

4) Watch the nozzle: when filament comes out normally, choose Done. If nothing comes out, choose Retry and repeat.

Load + Unload With the AMS

FILAMENTSTEP 24

Unload

Controls ▸ Feeding ▸ Unload, pull out

Same filament, 2 slots

26 / 29

AMS printers: put the roll on the AMS holder and push the loose end into the slot feeder until the AMS grabs it; it draws the filament in by itself.

If two slots hold the same filament type and colour, the AMS switches to the other one automatically when the first runs out.

Unload: on the printer screen Controls > Feeding > Unload retracts the filament into the AMS; then pull the end out of the feeder and lift the roll off.

Note: with SD card only, Studio cannot see what is in the AMS, so put the white matte PLA in the slot Studio expects. Verify the exact AMS menu wording on the real printer.

Filament Ran Out?

FILAMENTSTEP 25

Same colour as before

27 / 29

When the filament runs out the printer pauses by itself and remembers where it was.

1) Load the new filament (Load Filament slide for a non-AMS printer, or fill the AMS slot).

2) Check the nozzle is pushing plastic out.

3) Press Resume on the pop-up / printer screen and the print continues.

If the pop-up has gone, the pause button on the printer brings it back. Some users report a small layer shift after a runout, so check the next few layers.

If Something Goes Wrong

File not on the printer?

Is it .gcode.3mf? Card pushed in?

Lifting off the plate?

Wipe the plate, restart

Parts floating?

Re-arrange, check supports

Card unreadable?

Re-eject in Finder, retry

28 / 29

File missing: confirm it ends in .gcode.3mf and the card is fully seated.

First layer lifting: clean the textured plate (no fingerprints) and restart.

Floating parts: re-run Arrange and the Preview check.

Card unreadable: reinsert in the Mac, re-copy, eject properly.

ALL DONE
