Our Lab Rules [~ 3 min] Review this slide each week prior to the lesson content

Safety: hands off printers. The printer has a hot nozzle and moving parts that can pinch. The printer will keep moving, even if something is in its path.

Kind words and suggestions: everyone's design is their own artistic vision. Helpful ideas are welcome ('Have you tried...?', 'I like how...'), but we do not put down others designs.

Questions at the end: many of your questions will be answered in the lesson material. We answer any questions you still have at the end, this allows us to have more build time.

White filament: every print comes out white. Painting can be done at home or in week 6.

Stay on task: if you are off task you may be asked to go back to your classroom.

Locked items stay locked: some parts are locked on purpose so they stay the right size. Do not click the padlock to unlock them. If something will not move, it is supposed to stay put.

From Screen to Printer [~1-2 min]

Three steps

1. DESIGN: You build your idea on the computer in Tinkercad.

2. SLICE: A special program called a slicer cuts the design into hundreds of thin layers and writes step-by-step directions for the printer like where to move and when to squeeze out plastic.

3. PRINT: The printer follows those directions, one layer at a time.

How the Printer Builds It

1Plastic string called filament feeds in.
2The nozzle heats it until it melts, like a hot glue gun.
3The nozzle draws the first layer on the build plate.
4It moves up and draws the next layer on top.
5Repeat until it's done!
Hot and moving: look, don't touch!
7 / 15

How the Printer Builds It [~2 min]

Point to each part of the drawing as you go.

Filament is a long string of plastic on a spool. Ours is always white.

The nozzle gets very hot (hotter than an oven) and melts the plastic, just like a hot glue gun.

It draws the first layer flat on the build plate, then moves up a tiny bit and draws the next layer on top over and over.

Tie back to Rule 1: the nozzle is hot and the parts move fast, which is why we never reach into a printer. Note that even if it looks done printing, it may not be and the piece will still be hot.

We Measure in Millimeters [~2 min]

Hold up a ruler.

The tiny lines are millimeters (mm). One millimeter is about as thick as a dime.

Ten tiny lines make one centimeter (cm) — that's the numbered lines on the ruler.

Why mm? 3D printers are very exact, so they use tiny units.

Everything in Tinkercad is measured in mm, so learning this today helps us build next week.

11 / 15

How to Measure Like a Maker [~2 min]

Model this on the projection

1. Start at the 0 line, not the end of the ruler. Many rulers have a little blank space before 0.

2. Find the last numbered line you passed, then count the tiny lines after it.

Example: past the 3 (that's 30 mm) plus 7 tiny lines = 37 mm.

3. Always write the number AND 'mm’.

Labeling the unit of measurement is very important!

37 mm is very different from 37 inches or 37 feet.

Done Early? Start Your Free Design [variable timing] Put slide up as students are finishing the Measure It! activity

Introduce the Free Design Build. No paper needed: students start right in Tinkercad.

1. Log in to Tinkercad with the class code and their nickname. An adult helps with the first log in.

2. Click + Create (top right), then 3D Design. A new blank Workplane opens.

3. Rename it: click the name at the top left and type your name free design, like Stasey free design. This keeps it easy to find every week.

4. It must fit in 100 x 100 x 100 mm. Hold up a ruler to show how big 100 mm really is.

5. Each week, after the day's project is checked, open this same design from your dashboard and keep adding to it.

In Week 6 everyone finishes it. We print it and get it to you after our sessions end.

Same rules: kind words about everyone's ideas, white filament only, locked stays locked.

Clean up and preview next week: making a name keychain in Tinkercad.
