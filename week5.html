Set up

Before class: take supports off all prints. Sort each student's parts into a bag with their name.

Each bag: clicker base, 3 keycaps, 3 switches, key ring set, robot body, head and 4 limbs.

Cover tables. Paint pens, paper towels, and a drying spot for each student.

Paint first, assemble last, so the paint is dry before parts are handled and snapped together.

Our Lab Rules [~ 3 min] Review this slide each week prior to the lesson content

Safety: hands off printers. The printer has a hot nozzle and moving parts that can pinch. The printer will keep moving, even if something is in its path.

Kind words and suggestions: everyone's design is their own artistic vision. Helpful ideas are welcome ('Have you tried...?', 'I like how...'), but we do not put down others designs.

Questions at the end: many of your questions will be answered in the lesson material. We answer any questions you still have at the end, this allows us to have more build time.

White filament: every print comes out white. Painting can be done at home or in week 6.

Stay on task: if you are off task you may be asked to go back to your classroom.

Locked items stay locked: some parts are locked on purpose so they stay the right size. Do not click the padlock to unlock them. If something will not move, it is supposed to stay put.

Paint Pen Rules [~3 min]

Show a paint pen. Demo pressing the tip on scrap paper to start the paint flowing.

Paint first: everything gets painted before it is put together, so paint does not get into the switches or joints.

Keep joints clean: paint on a ball or in a socket makes the joint sticky and too tight to snap.

Clicker keycaps: fill the stamped dents with color, then wipe the top with a paper towel so the shape pops.

Thin and light: thick paint takes longer to dry and smears. Let one color dry before adding the next.

Assembly Day: Snap It Together

1. Switches in

Push each switch into the base until it clicks.

2. Keys on

Press each keycap onto the + on top of a switch.

3. Ring on

An adult adds the key ring. Click away!

4 / 5

Assembly: Clicker [~5 min] Paint must be dry

1. Switches in: the metal pins point down into the base. Push straight down until the side clips snap.

2. Keys on: line up the + under the keycap with the + on the switch and press straight down.

3. Ring on: an adult opens the small jump ring with two pliers, loops it through the tab hole and onto the chain.

Test it: every key should click. A loose switch: a drop of glue on the outside of the switch fixes it.

Assembly Day: Build Your Bot

1. Paint first

Color your parts with paint pens. Let them dry.

2. Head on

Press the head onto the neck ball until it pops.

3. Snap limbs

Pop each arm and leg into a socket. Push the head: boing!

5 / 5

Assembly: Robot [~10 min] Paint must be dry

Skip step 1 on this slide if the parts were already painted.

2. Head on: line up the socket under the head with the ball on the neck and press straight down until it pops on.

3. Snap limbs: arms go in the side sockets, legs in the bottom sockets. Push each ball straight in.

Test it: push the head down gently. The spring should push it back up. Every joint should swivel.

A ball that will not go in: check for paint or a support bit in the socket.
