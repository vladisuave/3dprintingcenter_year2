Our Lab Rules [~ 3 min] Review this slide each week prior to the lesson content

Safety: hands off printers. The printer has a hot nozzle and moving parts that can pinch. The printer will keep moving, even if something is in its path.

Kind words and suggestions: everyone's design is their own artistic vision. Helpful ideas are welcome ('Have you tried...?', 'I like how...'), but we do not put down others designs.

Questions at the end: many of your questions will be answered in the lesson material. We answer any questions you still have at the end, this allows us to have more build time.

White filament: every print comes out white. Painting can be done at home or in week 6.

Stay on task: if you are off task you may be asked to go back to your classroom.

Locked items stay locked: some parts are locked on purpose so they stay the right size. Do not click the padlock to unlock them. If something will not move, it is supposed to stay put.

Solid Adds, Hole Cuts [~2 min]

New concept this week: hole shapes.

A Solid shape adds plastic. A Hole shape cuts plastic away, like a cookie cutter or a stamp pressed into clay.

In Tinkercad, a Hole looks striped and see-through. That is how you know it is a hole.

Any shape can be a Hole: click it, then click Hole in the Shape panel.

Holes only cut when they are grouped with a solid (Ctrl + G).

Why holes and not solids: the keycap prints upside down, with the key top flat on the build plate. A raised design would sit under the cap with nothing to hold it up, so it would print in mid-air, droop or fail. A dent prints cleanly on the flat bed.

Bonus: a dent feels smooth to press, and it is easy to fill with a paint pen in Week 5.

Stamp Your Keys: 3 Steps [~5 min]

Optional LIVE DEMO on the projector while students watch. Chromebooks stay closed until after questions.

1. Pick: click a stamp from the kit and press Ctrl + D to copy it, so the original stays in the kit. Drag the copy NEXT TO a keycap.

2. Line up: Shift + click the stamp and the keycap, then press L. Click the middle dot on each flat line and the bottom dot on the up-and-down line. The stamp jumps to the center of the key top.

3. Group: Ctrl + G. The shape is now cut into the key.

Why next to, not on top: if you drag a shape onto another object, Tinkercad sets it on top. That puts the stamp on the open underside of the cap and nothing gets cut. The bottom dot puts the stamp back at the key top, which sits on the Workplane because the caps print upside down.

Repeat for each key. A key can also stay blank.

Stamp Helper [BACKUP SLIDE]

Put up for individuals as needed or place in stand ups on tables.

Stamp on top: the stamp was dragged onto the cap and landed on its open underside. Align with L and the bottom dot to put it back on the Workplane.

Letters: the caps print upside down, so letters must be flipped (M, then the left-right arrow).

Symmetrical letters (A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y) do not need it.

Ctrl + Z undoes the last step. Ungroup with Ctrl + Shift + G if needed.

Shift + drag a corner resizes evenly. Keep stamps at least about 5 mm so they show.

Before You Print: Keycaps Only

FOR ADULTS  The text, the base and unused stamps should never print with the keycaps.

1. Export selected shapes

How: Select the 3 keycaps, then Export → The selected shapes.

Why: Only the keycaps go to the printer. Use it every time you export.

2. Make the text a Hole

How: Click the text, then click Hole in the Shape panel.

Why: Tinkercad never exports a Hole that is not grouped, so the text cannot print by accident. Unused Hole stamps stay out too.

Check the first export in your slicer: only 3 keycaps on the plate.
10 / 11

Before You Print: Keycaps Only [ADULT PREP SLIDE]

Not for students. Use when sending keycaps to print after class.

The workplane is not the printer bed. Every solid object in the design exports, even if it sits off the workplane.

Method 1

Export selected shapes: select the 3 keycaps, then Export and choose The selected shapes. Purpose: only the keycaps print. This is the most reliable method.

Method 2

Make the text a Hole: click the instruction text, then click Hole. Purpose: Tinkercad does not export ungrouped holes, so the text can never print by accident. It looks gray and striped instead of red.

Because the stamps are Holes, any stamp a student did not use is also left out of the export.

Print the bases separately. They are all the same, so print them ahead of time.

Open the first export in the slicer and check that only the 3 keycaps are on the plate before printing a full center.

Assembly Day [~5 min] Use when the prints are back

Hand out each student's base, 3 keycaps and 3 switches.

1. Switches in: the metal pins point down into the base. Push straight down until the side clips snap.

2. Keys on: line up the + under the keycap with the + on the switch and press straight down.

3. Ring on: an adult opens the small jump ring with two pliers, loops it through the tab hole and onto the chain.

Test it: every key should click. A loose switch means the hole printed a little big. A drop of glue on the outside of the switch fixes it.
